🇬🇧 English Summary

In northern Kyushu, Japan, a stagnant seasonal rain front has caused extremely heavy rainfall, triggering the issuance of level 4 hazard warnings for landslides and flooding in Nagasaki and Saga prefectures. The Japan Meteorological Agency has issued an urgent 'linear precipitation band immediate forecast' for southern Nagasaki, indicating a high probability of this phenomenon occurring within the next three hours. A linear precipitation band is a sequence of developing cumulonimbus clouds that result in continuous, intense rainfall, significantly increasing the risk of life-threatening landslides and floods. In response, local authorities in Kashima City, Saga Prefecture, have issued evacuation orders for thousands of residents across multiple districts, urging all at-risk individuals to evacuate immediately. Citizens near cliffs and rivers are advised to monitor municipal evacuation information and their surroundings closely. This proactive approach highlights the severity of the weather event and the importance of timely disaster warnings and coordinated evacuation efforts to mitigate the risks posed by extreme weather patterns in vulnerable regions.
